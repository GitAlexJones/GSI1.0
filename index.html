Step 1: Allele frequencies (p and q)

Each locus has two alleles, A and a. p is the frequency of A; q is the frequency of a. They always add to 1.

p + q = 1  ⇒  q = 1 − p

Example: if p = 0.80, then q = 0.20.

Step 2: Genotype probability at one locus (Hardy-Weinberg)

Bull kelp sporophytes are diploid, with two copies of each gene.

⚠️ The #1 trap: don't forget the 2 in 2pq. A heterozygote can get A from mom and a from dad, or a from mom and A from dad, so there are two ways to make one.

Step 3: Combine across loci

Assuming loci are independent (linkage equilibrium), multiply the per-locus probabilities:

Region likelihood = P(locus 1) × P(locus 2) × P(locus 3) × …

Step 4: Compare and assign

Repeat Steps 2–3 for every baseline region. The region with the highest likelihood is the most likely origin.

🛑 Pro tips

  1. Use the right row. Pull p and q from the region you're calculating; don't mix Alaska's numbers with Oregon's.
  2. Watch your decimals. Products of small numbers get tiny fast. Keep 3–4 significant figures, or use scientific notation, so nothing rounds to 0.00.
  3. Labels don't change the math. A/a, 1/2 or B/b: p is always the first allele listed and q the second.
